# The Symmetry Group of the Binary Deletion Channel

**Target paper:** Francisco Pernice, *On the Symmetries of the Deletion Channel*, [arXiv:2202.03633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03633) (Allerton 2022), Sections II and IV, Conjecture IV.3 (and the stronger group conjecture $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}} = \tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathrm{BDC}} = \langle C, R \rangle$).  
**Companion files:** [`solutions/bdc_orbit_symmetry/explore.py`](explore.py), [`solutions/bdc_orbit_symmetry/proof.pdf`](proof.pdf).

## Verdict

- **Conjecture IV.3 and the stronger global group conjecture: PROVED.**
- **Exact classification (Theorem 1):** For every fixed deletion probability $d \in (0, 1)$, the global channel group $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}$ under Pernice's exact `defn:channel-group` is the Klein four-group
  $$
  \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d} = \{1, C, R, CR\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2,
  $$
  generated by bitwise complement $C$ and word reversal $R$.
- **Blocklength action and exact orbit sizes:** At blocklength $n$, the restricted group $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}^n$ has order $2$ for $n = 1$ and order $4$ for every $n \ge 2$. Every orbit of $x \in \{0, 1\}^n$ is $\{x, Cx, Rx, CRx\}$ (deduplicated), with cardinality $2$ if $Rx \in \{x, Cx\}$ and $4$ otherwise. In particular, every orbit has size at most $4$ (maximally $2$ for $n \in \{1, 2\}$, and $4$ for all $n \ge 3$, attained at $0^{n-1}1$).
- **No hidden assumptions on $h$:** Pernice's `defn:channel-group` does *not* assume that the output bijection $h \in \operatorname{Aut}(\{0, 1\}^*)$ is length-preserving or equals $g^{-1}$. Our proof derives both $h(A^n) = A^n$ and $h = g^{-1}$ globally directly from the channel support structure.
- **Endpoint cases:** At $d = 0$ and $d = 1$, $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}^n \cong S_{2^n}$ and the unique orbit has size $2^n$, confirming that $d \in (0, 1)$ is essential.

## 1. Setup and Reduction to Subsequence Counts

Let $A := \{0, 1\}$ and let $D = \mathrm{BDC}_d$ be the binary deletion channel that independently deletes each input bit with fixed probability $d \in (0, 1)$. Following Pernice (`defn:channel-group`, adapted from Polyanskiy, 2013, Section VI.A), the global channel group is
$$
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d} := \bigl\{g \in \operatorname{Aut}(A^*) : \exists\, h \in \operatorname{Aut}(A^*),\; h(D(gx)) \overset{\mathcal{D}}{=} D(x) \text{ and } |g(x)| = |x| \;\; \forall x \in A^*\bigr\},
$$
and $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}^n \subseteq \operatorname{Aut}(A^n)$ is the group of restrictions of elements of $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}$ to $A^n$.

For words $x, y \in A^*$, let $N_x(y)$ denote the number of increasing selections of positions in $x$ that spell $y$ (the subsequence multiplicity). For $|x| = n$ and $|y| = k \le n$, the exact transition probability is
$$
P_n(d)_{y, x} := \Pr\bigl(D(x) = y\bigr) = N_x(y)\, d^{n - k} (1 - d)^k,
$$
and $P_n(d)_{y, x} = 0$ for $|y| > n$. Since $0 < d < 1$, the support $\operatorname{supp}(D(x))$ consists precisely of all subsequences of $x$, and the unique length-$n$ word in $\operatorname{supp}(D(x))$ is $x$ itself.

**Lemma 1 (Output bijection forced; equivalence with subsequence-count preservation).** *Fix $d \in (0, 1)$. Let $g \in \operatorname{Aut}(A^*)$ be length-preserving and let $h \in \operatorname{Aut}(A^*)$ satisfy $h(D(gx)) \overset{\mathcal{D}}{=} D(x)$ for all $x \in A^*$. Then:*
1. *$h(A^n) = A^n$ for every $n \ge 0$, and $h = g^{-1}$ globally on $A^*$.*
2. *$g \in \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}$ if and only if*
   $$
   N_{gx}(gy) = N_x(y) \qquad (\forall x, y \in A^*,\; |y| \le |x|).
   \tag{1}
   $$

*Proof.* For each $n \ge 0$, every word $y \in A^{\le n}$ can be extended to some word $x \in A^n$ and obtained from $x$ by deleting the appended bits, so $\bigcup_{x \in A^n} \operatorname{supp}(D(x)) = A^{\le n}$. Since $h$ is a bijection with $h(\operatorname{supp}(D(gx))) = \operatorname{supp}(D(x))$ and $g(A^n) = A^n$, taking unions over $x \in A^n$ gives
$$
h(A^{\le n}) = h\Bigl(\bigcup_{x \in A^n} \operatorname{supp}(D(gx))\Bigr) = \bigcup_{x \in A^n} \operatorname{supp}(D(x)) = A^{\le n} \qquad (\forall n \ge 0).
$$
Taking successive set differences ($A^0 = A^{\le 0}$ and $A^n = A^{\le n} \setminus A^{\le n-1}$) forces $h(A^n) = A^n$ for all $n \ge 0$. Now, for any $x \in A^n$, intersecting $h(\operatorname{supp}(D(gx))) = \operatorname{supp}(D(x))$ with $A^n$ yields
$$
\{h(gx)\} = h\bigl(\operatorname{supp}(D(gx)) \cap A^n\bigr) = \operatorname{supp}(D(x)) \cap A^n = \{x\},
$$
so $h = g^{-1}$ globally. Substituting $h = g^{-1}$ into $h(D(gx)) \overset{\mathcal{D}}{=} D(x)$ gives $P_n(d)_{gy, gx} = P_n(d)_{y, x}$ for all $|y| = k \le n = |x|$; cancelling the strictly positive common factor $d^{n-k}(1 - d)^k$ yields (1), and conversely (1) implies $P_n(d)_{gy, gx} = P_n(d)_{y, x}$ for every fixed $d \in (0, 1)$ (including $d = 1/2$, where probabilities of different lengths can coincide). $\square$

## 2. Single-Deletion Reconstruction and Classification

**Lemma 2 (Single-deletion moment reconstruction for $n \ge 3$).** *For any word $x = x_1 \cdots x_n \in \{0, 1\}^n$ (viewing bits as integers in $\{0, 1\}$) and every $1 \le i \le n - 1$,*
$$
s_i(x) := \sum_{y \in \{0, 1\}^{n - 1}} N_x(y)\, y_i = (n - i) x_i + i\, x_{i + 1}.
\tag{2}
$$
*Consequently, for every $n \ge 3$, the single-deletion multiplicity deck $\{N_x(y)\}_{y \in \{0, 1\}^{n - 1}}$ uniquely determines $x$.*

*Proof.* Counting each of the $n$ single-position deletions of $x$ individually: the $n - i$ deletions at positions strictly after $i$ leave $x_i$ in position $i$, whereas the $i$ deletions at positions $\le i$ shift $x_{i+1}$ into position $i$. Summing the $i$th bit over all $n$ single-deletion outcomes gives (2). For $n \ge 3$, we have $n - 1 \ge 2$ while $x_2 \in \{0, 1\}$, so $s_1(x) = (n - 1)x_1 + x_2 \ge n - 1$ if and only if $x_1 = 1$. Once $x_i$ is known, $x_{i+1} = (s_i(x) - (n - i)x_i)/i$ recovers the remaining bits sequentially. (At $n = 2$, $01$ and $10$ share the same single-deletion deck.) $\square$

(See Manvel, Meyerowitz, Schwenk, Smith, and Stockmeyer, 1991, for the broader theory of sequence reconstruction from subsequence decks.)

**Theorem 1 (Classification of $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}$ and orbit bounds).** *For every fixed $d \in (0, 1)$:*
1. *$\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d} = \{1, C, R, CR\} \cong V_4$ (the Klein four-group), where $C$ flips every bit and $R$ reverses every word.*
2. *The blocklength-$n$ restriction $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}^n$ has order $2$ for $n = 1$ and order $4$ for every $n \ge 2$.*
3. *For every $n \ge 1$ and $x \in \{0, 1\}^n$, the orbit of $x$ is $\{x, Cx, Rx, CRx\}$ (deduplicated), of size $2$ if $Rx \in \{x, Cx\}$ and size $4$ otherwise. In particular, every orbit has size at most $4$ (at most $2$ for $n \in \{1, 2\}$, attaining $4$ at $0^{n-1}1$ for all $n \ge 3$).*

*Proof.* Both $C$ and $R$ are commuting length-preserving involutions satisfying (1): complement preserves the exact selected index sets, and reversal reflects selected index sets and reads them in reverse order. Thus $\{1, C, R, CR\} \subseteq \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}$.

Conversely, let $g \in \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}$. Its action on $A^1 = \{0, 1\}$ is either the identity or the swap; composing with $C$ if necessary yields a symmetry fixing $0$ and $1$. By (1) with $|y| = 1$, this symmetry preserves Hamming weight ($N_x(1)$), so on $A^2$ it fixes $00$ and $11$ and permutes $\{01, 10\}$. Composing with $R$ if necessary yields a symmetry $f \in \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}$ fixing $A^0 \cup A^1 \cup A^2$ pointwise.

We prove by induction on $n \ge 2$ that $f$ fixes $A^n$ pointwise. The base case $n = 2$ holds by construction. If $n \ge 3$ and $f|_{A^{n-1}} = \mathrm{id}$, then for every $x \in A^n$ and every $y \in A^{n-1}$, equation (1) gives
$$
N_{fx}(y) = N_{fx}(fy) = N_x(y).
$$
By Lemma 2, $fx = x$ for every $x \in A^n$. By induction, $f = \mathrm{id}$ globally on $A^*$, so $g \in \{1, C, R, CR\}$.

Finally, for $n = 1$, $R$ acts trivially while $C$ swaps $0$ and $1$, giving $|\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}^1| = 2$ and one orbit of size $2$. For $n \ge 2$, the constant words $0^n, 1^n$ separate $\{C, CR\}$ from $\{1, R\}$, while $0^{n-1}1$ separates $R$ from $1$, so $|\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}^n| = 4$. Since $Cx \neq x$ for every nonempty $x$, every orbit has size $2$ or $4$, with size $2$ iff $Rx \in \{x, Cx\}$ (automatically holding for $n = 2$, since $00, 11$ are palindromes and $01, 10$ satisfy $Rx = Cx$). For $n \ge 3$, $0^{n-1}1$ satisfies $Rx \notin \{x, Cx\}$, attaining orbit size $4$. $\square$

*Remark (Alternate subword-order proof).* Once Lemma 1 identifies $h = g^{-1}$, positive-probability transitions encode the subsequence partial order $y \preceq x$, so $g$ is an order automorphism of $(A^*, \preceq)$. By Halfon, Schnoebelen, and Zetzsche (LICS 2017, Lemma 3.8, [arXiv:1701.07470](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.07470)), every automorphism of $(A^*, \preceq)$ is an alphabet permutation optionally composed with reversal, yielding the same classification for $|A| = 2$.

*Remark (Endpoint cases and fixed-blocklength perspective).* At $d = 0$ ($D(x) = x$, take $h = g^{-1}$) and $d = 1$ ($D(x) = \varepsilon$, take $h = \mathrm{id}$), every length-preserving bijection belongs to $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}$, so $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{BDC}_d}^n \cong S_{2^n}$ and the unique orbit has size $2^n$; thus $d \in (0, 1)$ is necessary. Note also that Pernice's `defn:channel-group` is global on $A^*$. Even at a single fixed blocklength $n$, every $y \in A^k$ ($0 \le k \le n$) appears in $\operatorname{supp}(D(x))$ for exactly $\sum_{j=k}^n \binom{n}{j}$ inputs $x \in A^n$ (strictly decreasing in $k$), which still forces $h(A^k) = A^k$ and $h|_{A^n} = g^{-1}$; classifying standalone single-length channel groups at fixed $n$ is a separate question outside Pernice's global conjecture.
