\begin{abstract}
We examine Conjecture~\texttt{conj:spread} (\emph{Equilibrium spread}) of Dragonevskiy (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39341}{arXiv:2609.39341}).
First, we note candidly that the conjecture is underspecified in the source paper: no interval scoring rule, calibration-bettor information protocol, credibility level, or convergence limit is fixed.
Second, we refute the conjectured equality under natural formalizations of the stated pressures.
In the exchangeable Beta model against a public-information calibration bettor and any finite-budget logical trader, the unique minimax quote for every strictly proper binary loss is the zero-width singleton $[m, m]$ (spread~$0$, versus $19/20$ for every $95\%$ credible interval of $\mathrm{Beta}(1,1)$), and any single-bit settlement payoff depends on the posterior only through its mean $m$; in repeated play, sequential predictive means yield zero spread at every finite horizon.
Giving the bettor one extra private draw induces positive spread, but its width depends strictly on the scoring rule and bettor budget and is capped at $1/(2a+1)$ ($1/3 < 19/20$ for $a=1$).
Finally, by Walley's nonempty-core criterion, every singleton inside the natural-extension interval avoids sure loss; on $\varphi \Rightarrow A$ with $P(A)=1/2$, quoting $1/2$ on $A$ and singleton $q(\varphi)=1/4$ defeats all logical traders despite natural-extension width~$1/2$.
We contrast this with the proper interval score of Gneiting and Raftery~(2007), which recovers Beta equal-tailed credible intervals only by settling directly on the latent parameter $\theta$ at an explicit tail level~$\alpha$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and Specification Gaps}

In \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39341}{arXiv:2609.39341} (Sections~3--4), $W \subseteq \{0, 1\}^\Phi$ denotes the finite set of admissible truth assignments, $C := \conv(W)$ is the coherent polytope, and logical portfolios satisfy $\|x\|_1 \le 1$ with worst-world profit $\min_{w \in W} x \cdot (w - q)$. Proposition~\texttt{prop:walley} (restating Walley~\cite{walley1991}) characterizes sure-loss avoidance for interval credences $[l(\varphi), u(\varphi)]$ by existence of $p \in C$ with $l \le p \le u$, and defines the natural extension via the LP bounds $[\min, \max]\, p(\psi)$ over $\{p \in C : l \le p \le u \text{ on quoted propositions}\}$. Immediately following is:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Conjecture (\texttt{conj:spread}, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39341}{arXiv:2609.39341}, Section~4).}
\emph{Under a proper-scoring/\allowbreak coverage pressure (narrowing) opposed by logical traders and calibration bettors \cite{foster1998} settling on a verifiable anchor set (widening where unfounded), equilibrium spread width on $\varphi$ converges to the epistemic uncertainty consistent with the anchor and $\varphi$'s logical relations to it. In the exchangeable toy case (Beta posterior over a settleable event family), the equilibrium width equals the credible-interval width.}
\end{quote}

\begin{remark}[Underspecification in the source paper]
The paper leaves unspecified: (i)~the interval scoring rule or coverage penalty (experiment E-A1b uses a point Brier anchor $\mu(p - t)^2$ with $\mu = 0.5$, and E-A2 leaves spread-valued credences open), (ii)~the calibration bettor's information structure and utility, (iii)~the credibility level $1-\alpha$ and interval convention (equal-tailed vs.\ highest-density), and (iv)~the convergence limit. There is therefore no unique numerical equilibrium attributable to the paper's prose alone; Sections~\ref{sec:public}--\ref{sec:logic} test explicit, natural formalizations of the stated pressures.
\end{remark}

\section{Public-Information Beta Saddle and 1-Bit Non-Identifiability}\label{sec:public}

Condition on the public history, let $\theta \sim \mathrm{Beta}(a, b)$ ($a, b > 0$), draw $Y \mid \theta \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\theta)$, and write $m := \mathbb{E}[Y] = a/(a + b)$. The model posts $0 \le l \le u \le 1$. A public-information bettor chooses stake $x \in [-B, B]$ with settlement payoff
\[
G(l, u, x, Y) := x^+(Y - u) + x^-(l - Y), \qquad x^+ := \max(x, 0),\; x^- := \max(-x, 0).
\]
Because both truth values $Y \in \{0, 1\}$ are admissible, any finite-budget logical trader has optimal worst-world profit $0$ at every legal interval $[l, u] \subseteq [0, 1]$. Let $S(q, Y)$ be any strictly proper binary loss with finite Bayes risk $R(q; m) := \mathbb{E}_m[S(q, Y)]$, let $c > 0$ be a unit width charge, and set (absorbing any positive adversary multiplier into $B$)
\[
L(l, u, x) := \frac{1}{2}\bigl[R(l; m) + R(u; m)\bigr] + c(u - l) + \mathbb{E}[G(l, u, x, Y)].
\]

\begin{theorem}[Public-information Beta saddle]\label{thm:saddle}
For every $a, b > 0$, strictly proper binary loss $S$, width charge $c > 0$, and finite budget $B \ge 0$, the unique minimax quote is the zero-width singleton $[m, m]$, with optimal bettor stake $x = 0$ and game value $R(m; m)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $\mathbb{E}[G(l, u, x, Y)] = x^+(m - u) + x^-(l - m)$, maximizing over $|x| \le B$ gives $B\max(0, m - u, l - m) \ge 0$. At $[l, u] = [m, m]$, every stake yields zero expected gain, so $L(m, m, x) = R(m; m)$. Against $x = 0$, strict propriety of $S$ and $c > 0$ yield
\[
L(l, u, 0) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl[R(l; m) + R(u; m)\bigr] + c(u - l) \ge R(m; m),
\]
with equality if and only if $l = u = m$. Hence $L(m, m, x) = R(m; m) = L(m, m, 0) \le L(l, u, 0)$ for all admissible $(l, u, x)$, establishing both saddle inequalities and uniqueness of $[m, m]$ for $\max_{|x|\le B} L(l, u, x)$. The same argument holds for strictly proper midpoint scoring $R(\frac{l+u}{2}; m)$ plus any strictly increasing width cost minimized at $0$.
\end{proof}

For $\mathrm{Beta}(1, 1)$, every $95\%$ credible interval has width $19/20$ (the equal-tailed interval is $[1/40, 39/40]$), and every positive-mass credible interval of a proper Beta law has positive length, whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:saddle} gives spread $0$.

\begin{proposition}[1-bit non-identifiability and repeated settlement]\label{prop:nonident}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item For any fixed $(l, u, x)$ and integrable payoff $h(l, u, x, Y)$ settled on the next binary draw $Y$, $\mathbb{E}[h(l, u, x, Y)] = (1-m)h(l, u, x, 0) + mh(l, u, x, 1)$ depends on the posterior solely through $m$. For example, $\mathrm{Beta}(1, 1)$ and $\mathrm{Beta}(20, 20)$ share $m = 1/2$ while having $95\%$ equal-tailed widths $0.950000$ and $0.304396$.
\item In repeated settlement with public filtration $\mathcal{F}_t$ and predictive means $m_t = (a + \sum_{i=1}^t Y_i)/(a + b + t)$, quoting $[m_t, m_t]$ gives $\mathbb{E}[x_t(Y_{t+1} - m_t) \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = 0$ for every bounded $\mathcal{F}_t$-predictable stake $x_t$ (including forecast-bin calibration bets~\cite{foster1998}). Cumulative gain is a zero-mean martingale whose per-round average vanishes almost surely for uniformly bounded stakes (by Hoeffding--Azuma and Borel--Cantelli). Under additive per-round losses with exogenous draws, $[m_t, m_t]$ and $x_t = 0$ attain the saddle at every horizon, keeping the ratio of equilibrium spread to Beta credible width identically~$0$ at every finite~$t$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\section{Private-Signal Calibration Bettor: Score- and Budget-Dependent Cap}

Keep the same endpoint loss with $c = 1/16$ and start at a symmetric posterior $\mathrm{Beta}(a, a)$ ($a \ge 1$). Now add private information: after $[l, u]$ is posted, the bettor privately observes one extra conditionally independent draw $X \mid \theta \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\theta)$ before betting on $Y$. The conditional means $p_X = \mathbb{P}(Y = 1 \mid X) = \frac{1}{2} \pm D$ each have probability $1/2$, where $D := \frac{1}{2(2a + 1)}$, and the informed bettor's best response earns conditional expected profit $B\max(0, p_X - u, l - p_X)$.

For binary log loss, binary Brier loss $(q - Y)^2$, and negative spherical score $-[Yq + (1-Y)(1-q)]/\sqrt{q^2+(1-q)^2}$, the forecast slopes $g_S(d) := R'(\frac{1}{2} + d; \frac{1}{2})$ are
\[
g_{\mathrm{log}}(d) = \frac{d}{1/4 - d^2}, \qquad
g_{\mathrm{Brier}}(d) = 2d, \qquad
g_{\mathrm{spherical}}(d) = \frac{d}{(1/2 + 2d^2)^{3/2}}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Private-signal equilibrium]\label{thm:informed}
For each of the three scores above, any $a \ge 1$, $c = 1/16$, and $B \ge 0$, the unique subgame-perfect equilibrium quote is $[1/2 - d, 1/2 + d]$ with
\[
d = \bigl(g_S|_{[0, D]}\bigr)^{-1}\!\Bigl(\min\bigl\{g_S(D),\, \max(0, B - 2c)\bigr\}\Bigr) \in [0, D].
\]
Hence equilibrium spread $2d$ depends strictly on both the score $S$ and budget $B$, and is capped at $2D = \frac{1}{2a + 1}$ for all $B$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Clipping $(l, u)$ to $[\frac{1}{2} - D, \frac{1}{2} + D]$ cannot increase width or any profitable trade against $p_X = \frac{1}{2} \pm D$, while strictly lowering $R(\cdot; \frac{1}{2})$ on any moved endpoint because $R'(q; \frac{1}{2})$ has the sign of $q - \frac{1}{2}$. Every optimum therefore satisfies $\frac{1}{2} - D \le l \le u \le \frac{1}{2} + D$. On $|d| \le D \le 1/6$, all three scores are strictly convex: $g'_{\mathrm{spherical}}(d) = (\frac{1}{2} - 4d^2)/(\frac{1}{2} + 2d^2)^{5/2} > 0$ since $4D^2 \le 1/9 < 1/2$, and $g'_{\mathrm{log}}, g'_{\mathrm{Brier}} > 0$ directly. Expected optimal profit is convex in $(l, u)$ as an average of maxima of affine functions. The strictly convex model objective therefore has a unique minimizer, which is symmetric by invariance under $(l, u) \mapsto (1 - u, 1 - l)$. At radius $0 \le d \le D$, the optimized loss reduces to $R(\frac{1}{2} + d; \frac{1}{2}) + 2cd + B(D - d)$, whose derivative $g_S(d) + 2c - B$ and endpoint conditions yield the formula.
\end{proof}

For $\mathrm{Beta}(1, 1)$, the $95\%$ credible width is $19/20 = 0.95$ at every budget, whereas the informed-game spread is capped at $2D = 1/3$ and varies across scores below saturation:

\section{Logical Traders, Anchors, and Interval Scoring}\label{sec:logic}

\begin{proposition}[Anchor singleton defeats all logical traders]\label{prop:anchor}
Fix consistent anchor quotes with nonempty compact convex coherent set $K \subseteq C$, and let $[L, U] := \{p(\varphi) : p \in K\}$ be the natural extension at $\varphi$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item By Walley's nonempty-core criterion~\cite{walley1991}, quoting $[l, u]$ on $\varphi$ together with the anchors avoids sure loss if and only if $[l, u] \cap [L, U] \neq \varnothing$. In particular, every singleton $[q, q]$ with $q \in [L, U]$ defeats all logical traders.
\item Let $\varphi \Rightarrow A$ with verifiable anchor probability $P(A) = 1/2$. Then the natural extension of $\varphi$ is $[0, 1/2]$ (width $1/2$), while quoting $1/2$ on $A$ and the singleton $q(\varphi) = 1/4$ (width $0$) achieves zero guaranteed logical profit, zero expected gain for public anchor bettors, and every individual lower bound on model loss (proper score on $A$ plus width penalty on both quotes).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Part~(1) follows from convexity of $K$: $[L, U]$ is an interval, so $[l, u] \cap [L, U] \neq \varnothing \iff \exists p \in K$ with $l \le p(\varphi) \le u$. (Covering \emph{every} coherent extension requires the strictly stronger condition $l \le L \le U \le u$, whose sharpest solution is $[L, U]$.) For~(2), in coordinate order $(A, \varphi)$ the admissible worlds are $\{00, 10, 11\}$. Every distribution $(1/2, 1/2 - r, r)$ with $r \in [0, 1/2]$ extends $P(A) = 1/2$ with $P(\varphi) = r$; taking $r = 1/4$ witnesses coherence of $(1/2, 1/4)$ against all logical traders, and all such laws generate identical anchor observations under independent repetition, preserving the width gap ($0$ vs.\ $1/2$) at every horizon.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[When credible widths are elicited]
To elicit an equal-tailed $(1 - \alpha)$ credible interval for a target $Z \sim F$, one must fix $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ and score $[l, u]$ directly against $Z$ with the interval score of Gneiting and Raftery~\cite[Section~6.2]{gneiting2007}:
\[
S_\alpha(l, u; Z) = (u - l) + \frac{2}{\alpha}\bigl[(l - Z)_+ + (Z - u)_+\bigr],
\]
whose expected partial derivatives $-1 + 2F(l)/\alpha$ and $1 - 2[1 - F(u)]/\alpha$ uniquely select $[F^{-1}(\alpha/2), F^{-1}(1 - \alpha/2)]$. Setting $Z = \theta$ recovers the Beta equal-tailed credible interval, confirming that settling on the latent parameter $\theta$ and fixing a tail level $\alpha$ supply essential structure absent from a single-bit anchor game.
\end{remark}

\section{Reproducible Verification}

The script \texttt{solutions/no\_arbitrage\_lm/explore.py} independently minimizes over unrestricted endpoints $(l, u)$ using linear epigraph constraints for every pure contingent trading strategy (\texttt{SLSQP}) across 126 public-information cases and 63 private-signal cases, matching Theorems~\ref{thm:saddle} and~\ref{thm:informed} with maximum endpoint discrepancy $4.8 \times 10^{-7}$. It also solves two anchor extrema LPs (natural extension $[0, 0.5]$) and three primal logical-trader LPs (verifying profit $0$ at $[0, 0.5]$ and singleton $[0.25, 0.25]$, vs.\ unit-budget guaranteed profit $0.05$ at the disjoint quote $[0.6, 0.7]$).
